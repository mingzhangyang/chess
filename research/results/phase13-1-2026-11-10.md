## Three measured bottlenecks and Phase 13.2 scopes

### 1. Move generation and legality — 1,931.51 ms, 31.19%

- Paths: `Kernel.minimax → MovePicker.next/reset → SearchPosition.generateMoveCodes/generateQuietMoveCodes/legalMoveCode → generateFrom → addCandidate`; `Kernel.quiescence → generateMoveCodes`, plus `hasLegalMove` when no captures. Attack tests run through `isInCheck`, `isSquareAttacked` and `isSquareAttackedVacating`. Files: `src/utils/chess-ai/searchPosition.ts`, `independent/movePicker.ts`, callers in `independent/kernelSearch.ts`.
- Actual self contributors: `generateFrom` 775.35 ms/12.52%, `generateMoveCodes` 364.39/5.88%, `isSquareAttacked` 246.48/3.98%, vacating king test 211.15/3.41%. The large generator wrapper self share may contain inlined child work; do not count it twice with inclusive call paths.
- Cause: repeated per-piece/per-direction traversal, candidate filtering and king legality tests across the large main/Q tree. Tactical and quiet stages may revisit pieces; the no-capture Q path also asks for move existence. Source inspection establishes the path, but this profile does not measure exact call counts or prove that any individual invocation is redundant.
- Existing mechanisms: occupancy/officer/slider bitboards, tactical nearest-blocker scans, check/pin/evasion facts cached per stack depth, integer buffers, lazy staged picker, and targeted stored-move generation already exist. Do not propose these as new work; avoid replacing everything with a new generator.
- First independent review scope: specialize hot tactical/quiet candidate traversal and reduce repeated field/mode work inside `generateFrom`, preserving exact move order and legality; only change generator internals and differential tests. Measure before selecting a particular rewrite. Keep search/pruning, picker ordering and weights fixed.
- Correctness risks: pin/check evasion, king vacating attacks, en passant discovered checks, castling, promotion order, and tie order changing the search tree. Require perft, frozen generation order, random legal-set differential, make/undo restoration, evaluator parity and golden root scores/counters. Include current default-feature fixed-depth snapshots as well as reference-feature golden tests.
- Performance impact: it is the largest measured module during the 400 ms searches that reach 7/11; the deeper fixed-tree controls cost 190.21/259.67 ms. No ablation was performed, so no observed ms reduction or extra completed ply is attributed to this module alone.
- Hypothesis/acceptance proposal: 20–30% less module cost would give roughly 6–9% overall saving under unchanged work (Amdahl assumptions), not 31% overall acceleration. Target ≥8% median time reduction on a broader paired, alternating, ≥20-position suite, identical nodes/Q/root scores, ≤3% p95 regression, and no loss of median completed depth at 400 ms. The two current deeper fixtures should also improve, without being the only acceptance cases. These are proposed gates, not proven gains.

### 2. Evaluation — 1,430.05 ms, 23.09%

- Paths: `Kernel.minimax/quiescence → Kernel.evaluate → KernelEvaluator.evaluate → loadPawns, per-officer terms, kernelMobility/kernelKingSafety, passed-pawn distance/rook terms`. File: `independent/kernelEvaluation.ts`, shared helpers in `chessAIEvaluation.ts`.
- `KernelEvaluator.evaluate` self 902.83 ms/14.58% (largest individual function); `kernelKingSafety` 225.36/3.64%. Mobility/king-zone helpers can be inlined; low or absent standalone self samples do not establish that mobility is free. Pawn structure and passed-pawn helpers are included in the module total.
- Cause: per-evaluation officer enumeration, rook-file setup, both king safety computations and dynamic passed-pawn/king relationships remain necessary even when pawn structures hit. Main static evaluation and Q stand-pat both invoke it.
- Existing: incremental material/PSQT/phase/non-pawn/bishop/pawn masks in SearchPosition, 4,096-entry 256 KiB collision-verified pawn table, bitboard mobility and king zones, reused scratch arrays. Pawn hit rate was not instrumented in this run; do not invent one or add a duplicate pawn cache.
- Independent scope: profile line-level per-officer loops and pawn-table hit unpacking; reduce repeated exact calculations/scratch work, and only cache terms with all dependencies represented. Keep tuned weights and rounded arithmetic identical. King safety depends on officers, occupancy and king position and cannot be cached by pawn key alone.
- Risks: stale cache after make/undo, evaluation perspective/tuning dependence, rounding/tempo differences. Require bitboard/evaluator differential tests, incremental restoration, tuned evaluation tests and full root-score/node golden controls.
- Impact and gate: evaluation is frequent in both observed 7/11 searches, but no isolated time-to-depth contribution or extra ply has been experimentally established. Hypothesis: 15–25% evaluation saving could save about 3–6% overall; target ≥5% paired fixed-tree improvement over broad positions, unchanged scores/nodes, no >3% p95 regression, with equal-time depth as a secondary measure.

### 3. Make/undo and repetition — 950.27 ms, 15.34%

- Paths: `Kernel.minimax/quiescence → SearchPosition.makeMoveCode → applyMove → prepareFrame/setSquare/updateTerms → RepetitionStack.push`, then `SearchPosition.undo`. Files: `searchPosition.ts`, `searchPositionRepetition.ts`.
- `applyMove` self 258.72 ms/4.18%, repetition `push` 197.50/3.19%; `setSquare`, `updateTerms`, frame snapshots and undo contribute the rest. This is sampled self work, not overlapping inclusive sums.
- Cause: update hashes, incremental eval, multiple occupancy/slider bitboards, clocks and castling/EP state; save/restore undo fields; each repetition push copies the exact 64-square board and scans same-side entries in the reversible window. Exact board comparison protects against hash collision.
- Existing: reused typed arrays and undo frames, O(1) window signatures, irreversible-window reset, same-side scan, incremental evaluation and direct snapshot restoration. Do not propose hash-only repetition as an equivalent replacement.
- Independent scope: reduce repeated indexing/field copying and frame bookkeeping while preserving every restored field and collision-verified repetition identity. Consider a compact exact frame layout only after profiling it separately; avoid broad copy-make rewrite in the first change.
- Risks: rare EP/castling/null-move restoration, repetition completeness/signature and draw-boundary errors. Require hash-collision/repetition, history completeness, null/undo, random make/undo invariants and golden search counters.
- Impact and gate: the fixed deeper trees contain 113,810 and 197,161 visits; transition costs recur across them, but no causal per-visit saving or ply gain was measured here. Hypothesis: 20–30% module saving could save about 3–5% overall. Proposed gate ≥3% paired exact-tree improvement with zero restoration/parity failures, no >3% p95 regression; profile retained to confirm work did not merely shift elsewhere.

Search bodies/other tree work are 14.41%; SEE 4.73%, picker 3.10%, ordering 1.01%, TT 3.33% and GC 0.61%. PVS/alpha-beta and Q are visible in the top functions; LMR and TT probes may be inlined into minimax. These are not absent or cost-free. No evidence here justifies changing LMR/PVS/pruning or adding a new TT as the first intervention. The low initializer self share does not measure TT memory allocation latency precisely.

Pure execution work must preserve the same search tree and reduce elapsed time. Search-efficiency work changes the tree/ordering and needs separate node/depth and correctness review. Playing-strength work requires prospective match evidence at equal time; neither visits/s nor an extra completed ply proves Elo. Phase 13.2 should begin with one exact generator change, then independently review evaluator and state-transition scopes if measurements support them. No Phase 13.2 implementation or Phase 12.4 match rerun was performed.
